# Plants and climate (quick reference)

Sizes are typical mature height × width; they vary with climate and pruning. Always confirm with a local nursery. Use the Latin name when shopping.

Toxicity notes come from the ASPCA plant list (aspca.org, checked 2026-11-11) for pets; for children, treat any plant marked toxic as "not where small children play".

## Climate types to recognise

| Climate (Köppen idea) | Examples | What it means for plants |
|---|---|---|
| Hot semi-arid / dry, windy | Baku, Absheron, Sumgait | Hot dry summers, mild winters, strong wind, salty air near the sea, sandy soil. Drought and wind tolerant plants, mulch, drip irrigation. |
| Humid subtropical | Lankaran, Astara | Wet, mild. Many shrubs and citrus grow well; watch drainage and fungus. |
| Warm continental / dry summer | Ganja, Shamakhi lowlands, central plain | Hot summers, cold snaps in winter. Hardy Mediterranean plants and fruit trees. |
| Cool mountain | Guba, Gusar, Sheki uplands, Lerik | Cold winters, snow. Hardy shrubs, perennials and fruit trees; frost-tender plants in pots indoors over winter. |

Outside Azerbaijan, ask for the city and use the USDA hardiness zone or the local climate type.

| Plant | Size (H × W) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hosta (Hosta) | 0.4 × 0.6 m | Needs water; not for dry Absheron shade; **toxic** to dogs and cats (ASPCA) ⚠ |

| Hydrangea (Hydrangea macrophylla) | 1.2 × 1.2 m | Humid areas like Lankaran; **toxic** to dogs and cats (ASPCA) |
| Boxwood (Buxus sempervirens) | 0.5–2 m | Low hedges; **toxic** to dogs and cats (ASPCA), low toxicity for people |
| Fern (Dryopteris filix-mas) | 0.8 × 0.8 m | Moist shade; **poisonous if eaten** ⚠, not in a kids/pets play area |

For a kids/pets yard in shade, safer picks are hard to confirm; say "check with a local nursery" and keep ⚠ plants out of the play zone.

## Edible corner (6+ hours of sun)

Tomato, pepper, aubergine, cucumber, herbs (basil, dill, coriander, parsley, mint in a pot because it spreads), strawberries, a dwarf apple or apricot on dwarf rootstock (2.5–3 m).

## Lawn or not

A lawn needs a lot of water in dry climates. Alternatives: gravel with clumps of grasses and herbs, clover or thyme ground cover, a small lawn only where people sit or play.

## Seasonal calendar (hot-summer climate like Baku; shift by 2–4 weeks for mountains)

| Season | Jobs |
|---|---|
| Late winter (Feb–Mar) | Prune fruit trees and roses, plan, order seeds, fix fences and paths |
| Spring (Mar–May) | Plant trees and shrubs early, sow vegetables after the last frost, mulch 5–8 cm, start drip watering |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Water early morning, deep and less often; shade young plants; harvest; check facade for cracks after heat |
| Autumn (Sep–Nov) | Best time to plant shrubs and trees in dry climates, divide perennials, compost leaves, clean gutters |
| Winter (Dec–Jan) | Protect tender plants and pots from frost and wind, check damp on walls, clean tools |

## Watering rules of thumb

- Water deeply 1–3 times a week rather than a little every day.
- Early morning is best; never midday in summer.
- Drip irrigation saves a lot of water compared with a sprinkler.
- Mulch (bark, gravel, straw) 5–8 cm keeps the soil cool and moist.
