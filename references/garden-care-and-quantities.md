# Garden care and quantities (quick reference)

Rules of thumb, rounded. Dates are **approximate** (no official frost dates were found for Azerbaijani regions): always add "ask a local nursery or neighbours who grow vegetables". Show every calculation in one line.

## Vegetable sowing calendar (approx.)

Warm crops (tomato, pepper, aubergine, cucumber, beans, basil) go outside only after the **last frost** and when nights stay above about 10 °C.

| Region (climate) | Sow indoors (tomato, pepper) | Plant warm crops outside | Cool crops (greens, radish, peas, onion sets) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Absheron / Baku (dry, mild winter) | mid Feb – mid Mar | mid Apr – early May | Feb – Mar and Sep – Oct |
| Lankaran / Astara (humid subtropical) | Feb – early Mar | Apr | Feb – Mar and Sep – Nov |
| Ganja / central lowlands | late Feb – Mar | late Apr – mid May | Mar and Sep |
| Guba, Gusar, Sheki uplands (cool mountain) | Mar – early Apr | late May – early Jun | Apr – May and Aug – Sep |

- Indoor seedlings need 6–8 weeks before planting out; harden them off (outside a few hours a day) for 7–10 days first.
- Summer heat (Absheron, Ganja): lettuce and spinach bolt in hot months; grow them in spring and autumn, or in light shade.
- Outside Azerbaijan: ask for the city and use its average last-frost date (local weather service or extension office).

## Companion planting and rotation

Good pairs: tomato + basil or marigold; carrot + onion; beans + corn + squash; lettuce between slow crops; herbs at bed edges.
Keep apart: beans and peas next to onion or garlic; fennel next to most vegetables; potatoes next to tomatoes (shared diseases).

Simple 4-group rotation (move each group one bed per year; same family back in the same spot after 3 years):
1. **Fruiting** (tomato, pepper, aubergine, potato) → 2. **Legumes** (beans, peas) → 3. **Leafy and cabbage family** → 4. **Roots and onions**.

## Pest and disease check (from a description)

Ask: which plant, which part, what it looks like, since when, weather, watering. Then give the most likely 1–2 causes and the **least-toxic fix first**. Unsure → "take a photo or leaf to a local nursery or agronomy service".

| Sign | Likely cause | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sticky leaves, tiny green/black insects on tips | Aphids | Strong water spray; soapy-water spray (mild liquid soap); check every 3 days |
| Pale speckled leaves, fine webs, hot dry weather | Spider mites | Wash leaves; raise humidity; remove bad leaves |
| White powder on leaves | Powdery mildew | More air between plants; water the soil, not the leaves; remove worst leaves |
| Brown spots with rings on lower tomato leaves | Early blight (fungus) | Remove lower leaves; mulch; don't wet leaves; rotate next year |
| Black sunken end on tomatoes | Blossom-end rot (uneven water) | Deep, regular watering; mulch |
| Lower leaves yellow, plant pale | Too much water, or low nitrogen | Check soil 5 cm deep; feed with compost |
| Wilting in wet soil (Lankaran, clay) | Root rot / poor drainage | Raise the bed; improve drainage; water less |
| Holes in leaves, slime trails | Snails and slugs | Pick at dusk; beer traps; copper or grit barrier |

Chemical sprays: only if the simple fix fails, only products sold for that use, **follow the label**, keep children and pets away, never spray in wind or heat. Never mix products.

## Soil

- Test: a cheap pH kit, plus the "squeeze test" (sandy falls apart, clay forms a sticky ball).
- Most vegetables like pH 6–7. Shrubs from the Mediterranean cope with slightly alkaline soil.
- **Sandy Absheron soil:** add 5–10 cm of compost each year, mulch, water deeply.
- **Salty soil or white crust** (near the sea, or from salty well water): grow vegetables in raised beds or big containers with bought soil and compost; choose salt-tolerant plants (tamarisk, oleander, rosemary); water deeply now and then to wash salt down. Test well water if unsure.
- **Clay (Lankaran, lowlands):** add compost, never dig when wet, plant on slight mounds.

## Watering amount and drip run time

- Vegetables in a hot, dry summer: about **25–35 L per m² per week** (= 25–35 mm), in 2–3 deep waterings. New trees: about 20–40 L once or twice a week in the first summer. Established drought-tolerant plants: water only in long heat waves.
- Check before watering: soil 5–10 cm deep feels dry.
- **Drip run time:** `minutes = litres per plant ÷ (emitters per plant × L/h per emitter) × 60`.
  Example: tomato needs 4 L, one 2 L/h emitter → 4 ÷ 2 × 60 = **120 min**, or two emitters → 60 min.
- Weekly water for a bed: `area × L/m²` → 6 m² × 30 = 180 L per week. Compare with the tank size.
- Rain tank sizing (rough): `roof area m² × rain mm × 0.8 = litres` (1 mm on 1 m² = 1 L).

## Material quantities

- **Gravel / mulch / soil (m³):** `area m² × depth m`. Path gravel 5–8 cm, mulch 5–8 cm, raised bed = length × width × height. Gravel ≈ 1.6–1.7 tonnes per m³; add 10%.
  Example: path 12 m × 0.9 m × 0.06 m = 0.65 m³ → **0.7 m³ ≈ 1.1–1.2 t**.
- **Pavers / slabs:** `area × 1.05–1.10` (cuts). Garden-path base under them: 10–15 cm compacted crushed stone + 3–5 cm sand (driveways and anything for cars: a builder sizes it).
- **Edging:** both sides of a path = path length × 2 (+5%).
- **Plants for a bed:** `bed area ÷ (spacing × spacing)`. Example: 4 m² of lavender at 0.6 m spacing → 4 ÷ 0.36 = **11 plants**.
- **Weed membrane:** area + 10% for overlaps.

## Balcony and container garden

| Plant | Pot size |
|---|---|
| Herbs (basil, parsley, thyme) | 2–5 L, 15–20 cm deep |
| Lettuce, spinach, radish | 15–20 cm deep, wide trough |
| Pepper, aubergine | 10–15 L |
| Tomato (tall) | 20–30 L + stake |
| Dwarf fruit tree, olive, pomegranate | 40–60 L |

- Pots need drainage holes; use potting mix, not garden soil. Containers dry out fast: in Baku summers water daily, mulch the top.
- **Weight:** wet soil is heavy (a 40 L pot can weigh 50 kg or more). Keep big pots few and close to the wall; for many large planters or raised beds on a balcony, ask the building manager or an engineer. Secure pots and trellises against wind on high floors.
- Never block a balcony drain or emergency exit; don't let water run onto the neighbour below.

## Privacy screen (by speed and cost)

| Option | Speed | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trellis + climber (grapevine, jasmine, honeysuckle) | 1–2 seasons | low | Needs a solid frame; grapevine gives summer shade |
| Reed/bamboo screen or slatted panel | instant | low–mid | Check fence rules and wind load |
| Fast hedge (e.g. privet, laurel, cypress) | 2–4 years | mid | Plant by **mature width**; some get very big |
| Tall planters with grasses | instant–1 season | mid | Good for balconies and terraces |

Avoid running bamboo planted in the ground (it spreads into neighbours' plots); use clumping types or containers.
